You Don’t Need the Harvard Card

What One of Shopify’s Top Executives Wants Every Student to Know

The Advantage Board · August 31, 2026 · Opinions

“The people who ‘make it’ usually aren’t the smartest people in the room. They’re the ones who can take hits without losing momentum.”

Jessica Williams is the Global Head of Brand Marketing and Partnerships at Shopify, one of the most powerful commerce platforms on earth. She has built creator campaigns with the likes of MrBeast, Emma Chamberlain, Cardi B, Serena Williams, Alix Earle, and Tom Holland. She has driven over a billion organic views in a single year. And she will tell you, with a smile on her face, that none of it came from a prestigious college degree.

“I didn’t have the H-card,” she says plainly– the Harvard pedigree that many students believe will make or break their future. What she had instead was an instinct for people, learned from her mother, and the willingness to keep betting on it.

Over the years, Williams went from studying at John Jay College of Criminal Justice, to working with troubled youth, to Visa, to Director of Brand Marketing and Partnerships at Coinbase. Eventually she made her way to Shopify, where she has spent the past several years driving one of the most culturally resonant brand presences in e-commerce. The through line? Taking somewhat basic platforms and building character on them through the audience.

With all the people she has met and helped bring to success, Williams has noticed a common factor running among every success story she has witnessed.

She points to five habits shared by her most successful founders:

1) They’re obsessively close to their audience. They listen, and they know what their customers care about before the data tells them.

2) They recognize that most breakout businesses start off scrappy, repetitive, and imperfect and they’re willing to stick with their projects anyway.

3) They understand that you could have the best product, business, or idea in the world but if nobody knows about it, it doesn’t matter. Getting eyes on your work is just as important as the work itself.

4) They have insanely high levels of resilience. Every founder has struggles, what sets the successful ones apart is that they take years of rejection, pivots, embarrassment, and uncertainty in stride.

5) They build and genuinely enjoy the process–tweaking, learning, fixing, iterating–not just the idea of having made it.

For young people specifically, the advice she gives is direct and somewhat countercultural: stop consuming and start making things. Post the idea. Launch the business. Start the newsletter. Send the cold message to someone you admire. Put your work into the world before you feel qualified to do so. “The internet rewards proof of work now,” Williams says. “You can learn more in six months of consistently making and shipping things than you can in years of overthinking.” That's precisely how her own career progressed. She simply began, without a prestigious degree or a marketing background. Someone called, she said yes, repeat. “Closed mouths don't get fed,” she says. “Climbing cringe mountain is part of it. You ask the uncomfortable question. You send the email. You walk up to the person. That’s how you get places.” She responds to cold messages now –from students, from young founders, from people trying to make a difference– because she remembers the climb and respects it in others. Most people assume that intelligence, resources, timing, and luck are what separate those who make it from those who don’t. Jessica Williams reveals another path, one that depends on nothing but will: be willing to climb cringe mountain. You have no idea what’s waiting at the top.
