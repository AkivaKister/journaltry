How the 'Sephora Kids' Trend Became a Costly Business Problem

Virality isn't always free publicity—sometimes it's a financial and reputational liability.

By Nathan Kohl · January 26, 2026 · Pop Culture

What began as a harmless TikTok joke has turned into a costly reality for the beauty industry. Videos of preteens swarming Sephora stores, testing luxury skincare, and filming content may seem like free publicity, but they expose a deeper problem: brands built for adults have lost control of who they're really selling to.

The rise of "Sephora kids" reveals how influencer-driven marketing, designed to maximize attention, can blur boundaries, damage products, alienate core customers, and ultimately turn virality into a financial and reputational liability.

From a business perspective, the situation presents several distinct problems. First, there's the physical issue: children testing and contaminating products they don't purchase represents direct inventory loss. Second, the presence of large groups of unsupervised preteens changes the in-store atmosphere for adult customers, who have reported feeling uncomfortable and leaving without making purchases.

Third—and most importantly for long-term brand value—luxury skincare brands derive much of their appeal from aspiration and exclusivity. When the face of a brand becomes a 12-year-old, that positioning erodes.

The marketing lesson is significant: attention is not the same as the right attention. TikTok's algorithm doesn't distinguish between the customers a brand wants and those it doesn't. Optimizing purely for engagement can bring you an audience that's entirely wrong for your product.

The Sephora kids phenomenon also raises deeper questions about social media and adolescent behavior. When platforms profit from engagement regardless of content, and when "haul" culture normalizes consumption as identity, the business consequences are really just symptoms of something bigger.

For young entrepreneurs thinking about marketing: your audience matters as much as your reach. Who sees you is as important as how many.
