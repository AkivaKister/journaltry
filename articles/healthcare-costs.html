The Cost of Health

How American Healthcare costs rocketed

Rafaela Shpigelman · June 26, 2026 · Opinions

As of 2023, 76.4% of American adults reported living with one or more chronic health conditions, according to the CDC. American health is deteriorating by the minute, with the CDC also reporting that some of the main causes of death in The United States are cancer, strokes, and heart disease. However, as the health of the American people continues to worsen, so do the prices of healthcare. The overwhelming costs of healthcare has made seeking help unaffordable for a large percentage of Americans. In the United States, illnesses, injuries, and diseases do not just mean pain, they also mean debt, high-costs, and even the risk of bankruptcy.

The Johns Hopkins Bloomberg School of Public Health has reported that The United States spends the most out of all developed nations on healthcare, and yet, Americans are spending much more on healthcare than other countries. Just because the price of healthcare is exorbitant and unaffordable, does not mean that the healthcare in America is necessarily better. In fact, International Citizens Insurance has reported that the 2025 CEOWORLD Healthcare Index has placed The United States at number 15 on the list for the world's best healthcare. To put that into perspective, The United States spends the most amount of money on healthcare out of all developed nations, and America is still superseded in ranking of best healthcare by nations like Taiwan, Japan, and Sweden.

According to Harvard Health Publishing, many people resort to denying or delaying necessary procedures and treatments due to their high costs. This results in many Americans being unable to receive the proper care they need out of their fear of risking debt. According to NIH, one big reason for the unaffordability of American healthcare is the lack of price caps. Most nations put a price cap on medical services, so the price of the same treatment is about the same in every clinic and hospital in that country, However, that is not the case in America. The United States does not impose price caps on medical treatments, so the same surgery or evaluation could be $500 in one hospital and $5000 in the other. Without price caps, patients are left oblivious to the prices they are paying after their treatment.

The National Institute of Health also noted the use of unnecessary tests and treatments that are conducted on patients which inflate the ending cost. American healthcare runs on a fee-for-service basis. This means that consumers are charged for every material used, every procedure run, and every test conducted, regardless of the outcome. Even if it did not help the patient, the patient still pays for it. This greatly inflates the price but does not always improve the health of the patient, often resulting in the patient wasting money on things they did not need.

It has been stated by The National Institute of Health that prices of medical treatments are often undisclosed until everything is already done. This lack of transparency results in high bills that patients did not sign up for. Additionally, The National Institute of Health noted that this prevents competition as it does not allow patients to evaluate costs when deciding which clinic to go to.

In addition, The Baumol Effect is a major factor in the current crisis of health. Named after economist William Baumol, this principle explains why prices increase in certain fields but productivity and output do not improve. According to The National Institute of Health, the medical field is labor-intensive, and simply cannot be replaced by technological advancement or improvement. For example, a surgeon's skill cannot be replaced by technology and is as crucial now as it ever was, even with the advanced technology we have. Wages across the country are skyrocketing due to increased productivity in fields like manufacturing. As a result, health care providers must now increase wages to attract talent. However, just because the wages are getting higher in health care, does not mean that the efficiency in health care is rising like that of the other industries in The United States. This results in the costs of healthcare steadily increasing, making them slowly unaffordable for a number of Americans. However, this is not the only problem, this is simply a fuel for the other problems that create the exorbitant healthcare prices we see today. For example, a lack of competition allows clinics and hospitals to raise their prices without pushback for clients.

Furthermore, the US healthcare system seems to be prioritizing the wrong thing. Instead of promoting health and wellness, American health care focuses on treating the sick rather than preventing them from getting sick in the first place. According to NIH, diabetes and heart diseases account for 90% of US healthcare spending. These illnesses are often preventable by lifestyle improvements and wellness treatments. However, The United States only reserves a small amount of money for public health and disease preventative services in comparison to other very developed nations.

Not only is American healthcare unreasonably priced, it is not always distributed fairly. According to Harvard Health Publishing, the access to health and primary care is uneven. Many of those employed by companies that give out free health insurance hold that insurance over the heads of their employees. This means that a layoff can limit if not completely take away a person's access to proper healthcare. In addition, health insurance companies rarely allow for expensive medications or treatments, even if they are life saving and necessary, until forms are filled out which are meant to justify the need for these treatments to the insurance company . These forms can take a long time to fill, especially if those who are ill are in constant pain and agony, resulting in people having to postpone their healing until they can fill out enough paperwork, if the insurance accepts it. Many are left with the decision between debt or illnesses when they are denied life saving medical care by insurance companies.\ Ultimately, health care costs in The United States are unreasonably and exorbitantly high. Many, if not most Americans are constantly struggling between the decision between healthcare or debt. If America spends the most money out of all developed nations on the healthcare of its citizens, the choice between debt and health should not be a choice those citizens should have to make.
