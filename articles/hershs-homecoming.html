Hersh's Homecoming: How a Chicago Teen's Legacy Is Nourishing a Community

The first kosher community fridge in the U.S.—open to anyone, built to honor a life cut short.

The Advantage · January 25, 2026 · Community

Hersh's Fridge, the first kosher community fridge in the U.S., was launched in Chicago to honor Hersh Goldberg-Polin z''l, a young man tragically murdered after nearly a year in captivity. Open 24/7, it provides kosher food to anyone in need—Jews, Christians, and Muslims alike—while upholding strict kosher standards.

Spearheaded by students from Ida Crown Jewish Academy, the project combines chesed (acts of loving kindness), community service, and leadership. It transforms a tragic loss into a living tribute that strengthens the local Jewish community and promotes dignity for those struggling with food insecurity.

The fridge is stocked through ongoing donations from community members, local businesses, and national organizations. It has become a gathering point—not just a food resource—drawing people from across the neighborhood who come to contribute as much as to receive.

For the students who organized it, the project has been formative. They've navigated logistics, community relations, and media attention, all while holding grief for someone many of them knew or followed closely.

That's the thing about chesed: it doesn't ask whether you're ready. When there is a need and you have the capacity to respond, you respond. The students of Ida Crown Jewish Academy did exactly that.

Hersh's Fridge is not a monument. It's not a plaque or a memorial fund administered by adults. It's something living—something that feeds people, connects neighbors, and keeps Hersh's spirit present in a way that a static memorial never could.

It is, in the truest sense, a homecoming.
