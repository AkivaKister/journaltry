REKINDLING HOPE: Painting Survives Devastating Synagogue Fire

When a raging wildfire tore through her Pasadena synagogue, Rebecca Guberman feared everything she loved was lost.

The Advantage · February 23, 2026 · Community

When a raging wildfire tore through her Pasadena synagogue, Rebecca Guberman feared everything she loved was lost. But her painting—an ode to her late Rabbi's unwavering light—emerged unscathed, shining as a beacon of hope, resilience, and faith.

The fire swept through the synagogue with the speed and indifference that wildfires are known for. In the aftermath, the building was a shell of what it had been. Torah scrolls, prayer books, decades of accumulated memory—much of it was gone.

But Rebecca's painting survived.

To those who saw it, the painting's survival felt like more than coincidence. It became a symbol: that even in the darkest moments, light endures. That the values and commitments a community holds cannot be consumed by fire.

For the congregation, the months following the fire have been a process of grief and rebuilding. Services have been held in temporary spaces. Donations have poured in from Jewish communities across the country. The painting has traveled with the congregation, displayed prominently wherever they gather.

Amid fire, destruction, and the shadow of antisemitism that seems to grow heavier by the year, one artist's creation proves that light endures even when the world falls apart.

The synagogue's rebuilding campaign is ongoing. But its spirit—as the painting attests—never left.
