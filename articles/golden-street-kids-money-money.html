High Schooler and Wall Street

The Golden Age is NOW

Sammy Obadiah · August 20, 2026 · Business

“Once you graduate college, everything will be different.” That’s what everyone in finance tells you and what everyone seems to believe. News flash - they’re wrong. This is the golden age to be in high school, since Wall Street is already changing. There is no doubt that over the next decade it will continue to reshape itself, but the real advantage belongs to the high schoolers who are watching this happen now, before the system catches up. By the time this generation reaches college, top schools will have rebuilt their curricula to teach what actually matters: AI literacy, data thinking, model validation, etc.. Students who understand the why behind this shift and who can see it coming while it’s still forming are the ones with an edge that the first wave of college graduates won’t. In fact, just in the past few years, NYU Stern launched a dedicated AI and Finance track, and Wharton's curriculum now mandates machine learning courses for undergraduates. The golden age of change isn’t after graduation. It’s right now. Great, so AI is already taking over the world. But what does that mean for us? Less jobs? Less work? More stress? Not exactly. Let’s explore some of the changes that are happening NOW. If you’re a finance guy, you probably know what awaits - the brutal hours of Investment Banking interns, the high-pressure environment of private equity, and constant competition to get bonuses or promotions in any finance-related career. However, don’t fret. Junior analyst work, like building models, data scrubbing, pitch books - this has already gone from a 20-hour burden to just a 2-hour task. It isn’t replaced by deletion, it’s replaced by efficiency. Obviously, this means that these oh-so-coveted junior roles are declining, but for students equipped with AI literacy and model validation skills, this won’t be as drastic as people assume. So in a nutshell, AI isn’t simply replacing Wall Street jobs, it’s compressing the grunt work and reorganizing the firms to be as efficient as possible. As I’m writing this, firms are actively restructuring headcount and creating entirely new role categories. Remember, this change is happening today - while today’s college graduates will have to deal with the adaptation, tomorrow’s graduates will be ready for what is to be. Even the big names(Goldman, JPMorgan, Citadel) who have the most established and long-standing systems and policies are already shrinking intake, while raising pay for those hired. Equity research shops are running with leaner teams, and the surviving junior roles require Python/SQL literacy, not just finance knowledge. A business school like Wharton or Stern will adapt for you, not the other way around. Okay, so my college will teach me how to do everything AI. But that will only help me with the junior roles, right? Wrong again. One thing that scares many incoming Wall Street employees is what’s called “The Middle Squeeze.” Simply put, this is where major banks reduce the mid-level management roles, prioritizing the juniors and the seniors at their company. This can be daunting, as it can lead to mass firings, slow career progressions, and high workloads for whoever stays. Teams that once had 20+ people in VP or director roles now run with only 10, and risk managers will no longer tell you to “run the model” - they’ll tell you to “validate the model” - different skill, different role. And if you’re staring at this and thinking “what the hell is validating a model,” then don’t worry - that’s why we as high schoolers are in the golden age. The big changes are already happening, and the educational shifts will follow. What about the senior roles? They just yell at people, right? Surely their jobs won’t change. It’s actually quite the opposite. Senior portfolio manager and research director roles are actually expanding, since AI will allow them to fully prioritize strategy and client management. For example, if you are an investment manager, using AI can allow you to take on larger books, and manage more capital, making you much more efficient. To sum it up, the work involved with generating insights is being overtaken, making room for the expansion of acting on those insights. Even outside of current roles, there is so much change happening already on Wall Street that people aren’t even realizing. New role categories, such as AI governance roles, have scaled incredibly over the last 18 months, with mega firms like BlackRock, Citadel, and Jane Street taking on and prioritizing employees who are skilled in this field. For these roles, an AI governance specialist audits the models that traders and PMs rely on, stress-tests them for failure modes, and ensures a bad algorithmic decision doesn't blow up the firm. It requires a deep understanding of both finance and machine learning - exactly the hybrid skill that's desperately scarce right now. Many companies compete heavily for those who are talented in architecting data pipelines and ensuring quality - both jobs that have become streamlined due to AI. The system is changing, and top colleges will catch up fast. However, that isn’t the whole picture. You have to set yourself up for what Wall Street is becoming. The good news? You already know what’s coming - that’s why it’s so great to be a high schooler. You are the only one with 4+ years before it’s time to enter a changing job market. Maybe your parent’s playbook is obsolete, but for those reading this, witnessing the change now, you have the ability as a high schooler to start earlier than everyone else; so learn the tools, understand the why, and embrace the change, and you will be the one with a successful career and many options. Not just jobs. Options.
