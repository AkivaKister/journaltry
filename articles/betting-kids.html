​‌‌‍‍​‍Betting Apps Are Targeting and Impacting Teen Sports Fans- And Nobody’s Stopping Them

The Silent Epidemic in Teen Sports Culture

Benny Isak · June 7, 2026 · Opinions

You don’t have to be 21 to see ads for gambling apps. They’re everywhere. Billboards, YouTube, TikTok… everywhere a teen has access to. A 2025 study found that children are “particularly vulnerable” to the perceived link between sports events and gambling. The ads are explicitly targeting those who are underage, with booming music, flashy visuals, and using celebrities they know and love to endorse their company. I believe that this isn’t accidental- teens are being purposefully targeted, and the system is failing them.

Ads are designed to feel fun and intriguing- this clearly appeals to young people. Teenagers today spend hours daily glued to their devices. Influencers and celebrities that teens follow daily are consistently promoting these companies, normalizing it for the teens. Celebrities such as Drake (Stake) and Kevin Hart (DraftKings) are being paid by gambling apps to promote them.

Studies show that when teens see a celebrity that they recognize in a betting ad, they assume gambling is safe and acceptable, just because someone they look up to says so. The sports leagues themselves are partnering with the betting industry; the sport and the bet are merging into one pastime.

In apps like DraftKings or Fanduel Sportsbook, age verification is virtually nonexistent. Once again, I believe that this is not an accident. Teens make stupid decisions, which the apps know. Nearly 3 out of 4 high school males admit to placing an illegal online bet. Teens can easily log in through a parent’s account or get older kids to place bets for them. This isn’t random. It’s widespread, including in close-knit communities such as our Jewish towns and schools.

The consequences are real. You can go through any high school around the NBA Playoffs, Super Bowl Sunday, or March Madness, just to find them habitually conversing about placing wagers. These habits stick. According to Hopkins Bloomberg Public Health Magazine, 15% of adults ages 18-34 reported concerning gambling behavior, compared to just 2% of those 55 and older. These are the people who started betting as teens!

Especially in the Jewish community, teen sports fans find it normal to be placing bets day in and day out. They’d win a bet, and keep going, with parlays and legs, over-unders… trust me. I’ve seen it myself. I have friends who enjoy betting. It’s fun! But, it’s never worth the consequences. Sure, some teens walk away unscathed. But the majority who start, never stop.

In fact, on Tuesday, March 24, 2026, an ESPN article was published documenting a lawsuit filed against Fanduel and DraftKings, which accused them of exploiting NFL-supplied data to produce an “addictive product.” The companies hired “VIP hosts” to encourage two Pennsylvania men and give them incentives to continue gambling, causing over $2 million in combined losses. The VIP hosts were giving perks like Super Bowl tickets, hotel stays, and champagne. One plaintiff even placed himself on a self-exclusion list, but still received messages from his VIP host encouraging him to keep betting.

At the bottom of the article, ESPN has a disclaimer stating that they themselves have business partnerships with DraftKings and the NFL, showing how deep these partnerships really go.

I think that these apps have malicious intent, to take advantage of people just so they can make some money. This lawsuit demonstrates that it’s not only teens that are being hurt- it’s the entire population.

I even know someone who won money their first time betting. They’ve been smart, and even won a large sum of money in the Super Bowl. But they’ve kept going. That's exactly how it's designed to work. The apps are designed in a way so that the companies always win in the long run. Offers of bonus bets, first bet insurance, promotions, and odds boosts are all engineered to keep you betting, like a casino that never, ever closes. I know, it’s tempting.

Teen brains aren’t developed enough yet to assess risk and long-term consequences, so they can’t help themselves. Studies done by the National Institute of Health using brain imaging found that when teens make decisions while being watched by peers, reward-related brain activity spikes significantly- meaning teens are more likely to take risks like gambling in social settings than when alone. The apps know this. Anxiety, depression, and missed school days that gambling can cause are not the teens’ faults- they were set up for failure, and it has to be stopped.

So, what has to change?

I think that there has to be stricter age verification on gambling apps. It’s for the good of all teen sports fans, even if the gambling just seems like harmless play. Parents are generally unaware if their child has a gambling issue, and they should set strict guidelines for their children. The apps profit from every bet placed, legal or not, so if nobody says something, then they won’t change a thing.

Responsible ad standards must be held- no more influencer promos targeted towards teen audiences. In fact, I think that there should be less advertising towards gambling in general. Those who can legally gamble, already know how. The ads are only hurting us teens.

Leagues such as the NFL and NBA should be held accountable for who they partner with. The reason they haven’t cracked down on teen gambling is simply the same reason the apps don’t- money. The NFL sells jerseys to children, the NBA markets to children. But they profit from betting partnerships that harm those same kids. That's not a coincidence, that's a choice they make.

We Jewish teens didn’t ask to be targeted. We didn’t ask to become addicted. But until these apps, leagues, and companies change their ways and are held accountable, nothing is going to change. You don’t have to be 21 to access a gambling app these days, but you shouldn’t have to be 21 to be protected from one either.
