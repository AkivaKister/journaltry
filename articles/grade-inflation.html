The Effort Gap

Students are more capable than they think - and it's time they started acting like it.

Benny Isak · June 25, 2026 · Opinions

Finals week. A time when students around the country need to get a good grade in order to uphold their report card. But, walk into a high school, and you’ll feel something missing: urgency. Somewhere along the way, students stopped putting in the effort, and the world just accepted it. And yet, it’s become the norm. When did a 97 on a test or a 4.0 GPA become something that has to be bragged about?

I myself try to put in the work. I’ve found that putting in the effort pays off more often than not. But, I can’t say the same for many of my peers. And, I don’t think that getting work done is something that they forget about. It’s something that they just don’t want to do and don’t understand the importance of.

The data suggests that students are capable, but capable and actually achieving something mean two different things. In fact, the average GPA in the US is rising. According to the NCES, the average high school GPA soared from 2.68 in 1990, to 3.00 in 2009, to 3.11 in 2019. That's a 78 to an 85 to an 86!

But we don’t have to look far to see this disconnect. Right here in our own communities, in the 2024-25 school year, the Englewood Public School district had a Math proficiency rate of 20.7% and an ELA proficiency rate of 40.4%. Any educator would consider that abysmal. These numbers represent less than ⅕ of students meeting expectations in Math, and fewer than half of students meeting expectations in ELA - in one of the wealthiest counties in the country.

And even in a higher-performing district like Edgewater, Math proficiency sits at 60.1% and ELA at 63.4%, still well below New Jersey’s target of 80%. These rates are occurring in our communities! GPA scores may be going up, but real skill and mastery is going down. That’s not what I would consider improvement.

And, this decline isn’t just a data set coming from education departments. Gallup, a leading research and analytics firm, sends out a survey every year called the Gallup Student Poll, which asks students about their school experiences. According to a Gallup article, 25%-54% of Gen Z students, from kindergarten to 12th grade, are lacking engaging school experiences. They admit to not being totally engaged, and this is also a reflection of the schools education systems.

The attendance reports show similar engagement results. Chronic absenteeism is when students miss 10% or more of school. The USDOE Chronic Absenteeism Record is a record of how many students have hit this attendance rate. The record states that the U.S. rate of chronic absenteeism reached 31% in the 2021-22 school year and 28% in the 2022-23 school year. Chronic absenteeism can’t be the new norm either! School systems are doing what they can to help schools and communities increase attendance rates, but they can only do so much.

Schools may lack engaging learning experiences, and that has to change as well. The Gallup Student Poll found that nearly half of students disagree that they are having engaging experiences. They may feel optimistic for college and the rest of their life, but are not really prepared for it.

Yes, schools can do better, but it’s ultimately up to the students. Even in broken school systems, there is always a way to push through and perform. Conversely, even in exceptional school systems, there is always a way to not meet expectations and underperform. Clearly, the student’s performance is a partially independent variable from the school.

I find that even when certain teachers lack engaging learning, it is still up to the students to figure it out. Teachers themselves may even make students “learn on their own” from time to time. The blame shouldn’t be put on the schools. A 4.0 should be the floor, not something that people look up to and think they can’t achieve on their own. They can do it! It just takes time and effort.

There are many issues that have to be improved in today’s age, but this is one of the most overlooked. People say that one bad grade doesn’t matter that much, but think about it. It does. Failing a test can lower your average enough to significantly hurt your final grade. In order to feel confident about grades and get into a college you want, effort is a must.

The cause and effect is clear. Effort almost always leads to a good outcome. And if the effort is there but the grade isn’t, teachers notice and adjust accordingly. It’s an experience I’ve gone through myself.

Kids must be taught a good work ethic from a young age. Even so, urgency can't necessarily be taught - it’s a choice. The ones who put in the work and effort will make that clear soon enough.
